Major online platforms increasingly rely on large language models to condense product reviews into short summaries that, for most shoppers, substitute for the reviews themselves. We construct review corpora with known sentiment distributions and score both reviews and LLM-generated summaries using a BERT-based rating model and lexicon-based sentiment analysis. We document two distortions: centralization pulls summary ratings toward the midpoint of the scale, obscuring the signal of polarized products and suppressing strong praise more than strong criticism; sentiment dampening reduces the lexical strength of sentiment, compressing the emotional extremes of the review distribution. A survey experiment on simulated product pages links these distortions to consumer purchase intentions, highlighting faithfulness as a central design criterion for AI-mediated information.
