Complementor multihoming is often viewed as weakening platforms by reducing differentiation and increasing consumer substitution. Using a unique dataset comprising seven years of data on thousands of creators on Douyin, the Chinese version of TikTok, and RedNote, we provide the first empirical evidence on creator multihoming behavior and its consequences. Creators tend to adopt an additional platform after growth slowdowns and strategically cross-post selected content. Exploiting staggered platform adoption in a difference-in-differences design, we find that adopting a second platform increases followers and engagement on the focal platform. Mechanism analyses show that the gains are largely driven by audience expansion through cross-platform attention spillovers, with implications for creator strategy and platform governance.
