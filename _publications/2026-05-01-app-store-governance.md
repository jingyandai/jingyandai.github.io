We examine how a platform's exercise of control affects complementors' supply decisions. Leveraging Apple's App Store Improvements policy, a 2022 enforcement wave that notified developers of outdated apps of impending removal, as a natural experiment, we compare more than 70,000 iOS apps against Android counterparts. The policy stimulated developers to update long-neglected apps, but ratings volume and the share of positive ratings subsequently declined, suggesting that governance pressure can achieve its immediate goal while imposing collateral costs on perceived quality.
